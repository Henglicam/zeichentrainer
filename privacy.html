Privacy policy

识字 Shízì · Last updated 2 October 2026

识字 Shízì is a Chinese character trainer. It is built so that your cards and photos are kept on your device. Two things it sends by itself, and you can switch off both: a count of how much the app is used, and — to have the characters, the pinyin and the meaning checked and explained — the text of your cards, with a picture of the text for most photos. Buttons you press yourself can send the text of cards you already have as well; they are named below.

What the app stores, and where

Everything you create in the app — your cards, your photos, the cropped card images, your learning progress, and your settings — is stored only on your own device, in the browser's local database. The app has no user accounts and no sign-in, and keeps no copy of your cards anywhere else. The app's owner runs three small things on Supabase, each described below: the relay that passes your cards' text and pictures on to the AI provider without storing them, a table for the usage counts and a table for feedback. Beyond what this page lists, the owner cannot see your data.

What leaves your device

  • Text recognition and handwriting recognition run entirely on the device. Nothing is uploaded for them. The exceptions are the AI check of a card's text and the picture of a photo's text that the AI reads or checks; both are described below.
  • Usage counts (on by default, can be switched off under More → Usage sharing). Once a day, while online, the app sends anonymous usage counts to the app's owner — and once more when you leave the app after making or deleting a card, so that the day's cards are counted on the day. What goes: a random installation id, the app version, the phone model and system version as the browser reports them, the language your phone is set to, the date you first used the app, how many days the app was used, your day streak, the number of app opens, reviews, cards learned, cards made from photos, cards typed by hand and cards deleted, how many cards, photos and tags you hold, AI calls with their token counts, how many pictures were sent to the AI, how often the on-device reader and each AI model did the work, which AI provider and model is set up, how often a text on a photo was tapped, graded and turned into a flashcard, how many points you wrote on the pad, whether the app is installed on the home screen, and the app's last error messages (when, what failed and the message, with any Chinese characters removed). Most of the counts go both as a total and as a figure for the current month. No card text, no photos, no API key, no name, no account, no location. The counts are stored in a database table (Supabase) that only the app's owner can read, to see how the app is used. Switching the setting off stops the sending at once.
  • Online AI review (on by default, can be switched off under More → AI review). When a card is made or changed, the app sends the text of that card — the characters, the pinyin, the meaning, your review note, the word-by-word gloss, and the other readings the on-device reader produced from the same photo, never photos themselves — to an AI provider (for example DeepSeek, Alibaba, Zhipu, or Anthropic) to have the pinyin and the meaning checked; what the provider does with it is governed by that provider's privacy policy. Through the owner's relay the providers are DeepSeek and Alibaba (Qwen), companies in China, and the requests are processed there; the relay and the owner's two tables run on Supabase. The app's normal way to the provider is a small relay run by the app's owner (a Supabase edge function): the phone sends the request there together with the same random installation id as the usage counts, and the relay adds the owner's key and forwards it. The relay keeps a daily count of calls per installation and provider, and writes one short log line per call — the provider, the size of the request, the answer's status and length and how long it took, never the text — into the function's log on Supabase; the owner's key never reaches the device. Only the app's owner can enter an API key of a provider instead; requests then go straight from the device to that provider. These buttons send card text because you pressed them, not because a card changed: Check-up under More sends every card in the deck, Translate all cards every card whose meaning is in another language, Ask AI under More → Review queue the cards waiting there, and Ask AI in the card editor and in the character picker the card or line you are working on. While the switch above is ticked, the app also asks by itself: the character picker the moment it opens, the card form a second after you stop typing a new card's characters, cards still waiting for a real translation each time the app starts or comes back online, and — when you come to a card that has no description yet, in Learn or by opening it under Cards, or a card that has no meanings yet for its single characters in this word — the provider for them, a moment after the card appears and without waiting for you to open Details: the card's text goes out without a tap, and once more in a later session if no answer came. A multicard's texts get their descriptions the same way: when the multicard is made, and once when you open an older one, all its texts go out together — once with the multicard's title, for a one-line description each, and once for their longer descriptions. A Check-up or Translate-all run that was interrupted picks itself up again by itself when the app is next opened or comes back online — but only while the switch under More → AI review is ticked. With the switch off, an interrupted run simply waits until you press the button again.
  • A picture of the text (on by default, needs a provider whose model reads pictures). For most photos, the app sends a picture of the text — reduced to at most 800 pixels on the longer side — to a provider whose model reads pictures (Alibaba's Qwen, Zhipu's GLM, or Anthropic's Claude); through the owner's relay that is Qwen, so this works without a key of your own. It sends the picture when the app cannot read the text well on its own, when the photo seems to hold several separate texts (a board, a menu, a panel, a screen), and also when the phone's own reader is sure of the text, so that the provider can check that reading. No picture goes when the phone's reader reads a priced menu board surely — then only the board's texts go, as text — or when the photo is strongly tilted. It decides early, from a first quick look at the photo rather than from the finished reading, so on some photos it sends the picture and then turns out not to have needed it. Usually the picture is the part of the photo the app framed, but often it is the whole photo. That happens when the provider answers that the framed part holds no Chinese text, or that the text it found is only a very small patch of it: the app then opens the frame to the whole photo and asks once more. It also happens when the provider reports that the frame cut a line off at an edge — the app reaches out to the photo's own edge on every side it names, which is the whole photo when it names them all. And for a screenshot you shared into the app, or a photo from your album that the app recognises as a screenshot, the whole picture goes with the very first request and there is no second one. Unticking the switch under More → AI review stops the automatic check, every picture and every text the app would send by itself, so no photo of yours leaves the device. It does not switch off the buttons: Check-up, Translate all cards and Ask AI still send a card's text when you press them. It does stop an interrupted run of the first two from carrying on by itself: that run waits for the button.
  • Downloads. The app fetches its own files (the app itself, recognition data, the optional translation model) from its hosting (GitHub Pages) or a public mirror (jsDelivr). These are ordinary web requests; like any web server, the hosts may log standard technical data such as your IP address. We do not receive or store any of it.
  • Read aloud (on by default, can be switched off under More → Learning). When Learn reads the Chinese aloud, or you tap a speaker button, the app hands the characters to a text-to-speech voice your phone or browser provides, and it picks a voice that speaks on the device whenever one is offered. Only where the browser offers nothing but online voices — Chrome or Edge on a computer, for example — does the browser's maker (Google, Microsoft) turn the text into speech on its servers. The app itself sends nothing for this.
  • Feedback (only when you send it). A message you write under More → Feedback goes to the app's owner together with the app version and the same random installation id as the usage counts. It carries no name and no cards unless you type them. You may attach one screenshot with Add screenshot; it is reduced to at most 1400 pixels on the longer side and sent with the message, and nothing is attached unless you pick it.
  • Exports and sharing. Your data leaves the device only when you choose to export or share it through your phone's share sheet. You decide where it goes.

Analytics and tracking

Apart from the daily usage counts described above, the app contains no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no third-party scripts. The counts carry a random installation id, not your name. The same id goes with every request through the relay and with any feedback you send, so a message in which you name yourself can be linked to that id.

Deleting your data

Your data belongs to you and lives on your device. You can delete individual cards and photos in the app at any time. Removing all of it at once is best done by uninstalling the app or clearing its site data in your browser, which leaves nothing on the device. Usage rows and feedback you already sent stay in the owner's tables under your random installation id; to have them deleted, send that id — it is shown under More → Usage sharing — through More → Feedback, and the owner deletes every row that carries it. (The app also has a Start over button, but it sits behind the owner's password and is not meant for everyday use; it deletes your cards, your photos, your progress and the app's own diagnostic logs, and keeps the settings — the language, the switches, the installation id with its usage counts, and any API key the owner entered.)

Children

The app is a general-audience learning tool for adults. It does not knowingly collect any personal information — from children or anyone else.
