Privacy policy

街字 Jiēzì · Last updated 23 September 2026

街字 Jiēzì is a Chinese character trainer. It is built so that your cards and photos stay on your device. Two things it sends by itself, and you can switch off both: a count of how much the app is used, and — to have the characters, the pinyin and the meaning checked — the text of a new card, with a picture of the text when the app cannot read it on its own. Buttons you press yourself can send the text of cards you already have as well; they are named below.

What the app stores, and where

Everything you create in the app — your cards, your photos, the cropped card images, your learning progress, and your settings — is stored only on your own device, in the browser's local database. The app has no user accounts, no sign-in, and no server of its own. We cannot see, collect, or access any of your data.

What leaves your device

  • Text recognition and handwriting recognition run entirely on the device. Nothing is uploaded for them. The exceptions are the AI check of a card's text and the picture of a text the app could not read on its own; both are described below.
  • Usage counts (on by default, can be switched off under More → Usage sharing). Once a day, while online, the app sends anonymous usage counts to the app's owner — and once more when you leave the app after making or deleting a card, so that the day's cards are counted on the day. What goes: a random installation id, the app version, the phone model, the language your phone is set to, the date you first used the app, how many days the app was used, your day streak, the number of app opens, reviews, cards learned, cards made from photos, cards typed by hand and cards deleted, how many cards, photos and tags you hold, AI calls with their token counts, how many photos were sent to the AI, how often the on-device reader and each AI model did the work, which AI provider and model is set up, how often a text on a photo was tapped, graded and turned into a flashcard, how many points you wrote on the pad, whether the app is installed on the home screen, and the app's last error messages (what failed and the message, with any Chinese characters removed). Most of the counts go both as a total and as a figure for the current month. No card text, no photos, no API key, no name, no account, no location. The counts are stored in a database table (Supabase) that only the app's owner can read, to see how the app is used. Switching the setting off stops the sending at once.
  • Online AI review (on by default, can be switched off under More → AI review). When a card is made or changed, the app sends the text of that card — the characters, the pinyin, the meaning, your review note, the word-by-word gloss, and the other readings the on-device reader produced from the same photo, never photos themselves — to an AI provider (for example DeepSeek, Alibaba, Zhipu, or Anthropic) to have the pinyin and the meaning checked. That request is between your device and the provider and is governed by that provider's privacy policy. If you enter an API key of your own it is used instead, and it is stored only on your device. A device without a key of its own — the normal case — sends these requests through a small relay run by the app's owner (a Supabase edge function), which adds the owner's key, forwards the request to the provider, and stores nothing but a daily count per installation; the owner's key never reaches the device. Three buttons send card text because you pressed them, not because a card changed: Check-up and Translate all cards under More each send every card in the deck, and Ask AI in the card editor and in the character picker each send the one card you are working on. And when you come to a card that has no description yet — in Learn, or by opening it under Cards — the app asks the provider for one by itself, a moment after the card appears and without waiting for you to open Whole card: the card's text goes out without a tap, once per card, and only while the switch above is ticked. A Check-up or Translate-all run that was interrupted picks itself up again by itself when the app is next opened or comes back online — but only while the switch under More → AI review is ticked. With the switch off, an interrupted run simply waits until you press the button again.
  • A picture of the text (on by default, needs a provider whose model reads pictures). When the app cannot read a photo's text well on its own, it sends a picture of that text — reduced to at most 800 pixels on the longer side — to a provider whose model reads pictures (Alibaba's Qwen, Zhipu's GLM, or Anthropic's Claude); through the owner's relay that is Qwen, so this works without a key of your own. It decides early, from a first quick look at the photo rather than from the finished reading, so on some photos it sends the picture and then turns out not to have needed it. Usually the picture is the part of the photo the app framed, but often it is the whole photo. That happens when the provider answers that the framed part holds no Chinese text, or that the text it found is only a very small patch of it: the app then opens the frame to the whole photo and asks once more. It also happens when the provider reports that the frame cut a line off at an edge — the app reaches out to the photo's own edge on every side it names, which is the whole photo when it names them all. And for a screenshot you shared into the app, or a photo from your album that the app recognises as a screenshot, the whole picture goes with the very first request and there is no second one. Unticking the switch under More → AI review stops the automatic check and every picture, so no photo of yours leaves the device. It does not switch off the buttons: Check-up, Translate all cards and Ask AI still send a card's text when you press them. It does stop an interrupted run of the first two from carrying on by itself: that run waits for the button.
  • Downloads. The app fetches its own files (the app itself, recognition data, the optional translation model) from its hosting (GitHub Pages) or a public mirror (jsDelivr). These are ordinary web requests; like any web server, the hosts may log standard technical data such as your IP address. We do not receive or store any of it.
  • Feedback (only when you send it). A message you write under More → Feedback goes to the app's owner together with the app version and the same random installation id as the usage counts. It carries no name and no cards unless you type them. You may attach one screenshot with Add screenshot; it is reduced to at most 1400 pixels on the longer side and sent with the message, and nothing is attached unless you pick it.
  • Exports and sharing. Your data leaves the device only when you choose to export or share it through your phone's share sheet. You decide where it goes.

Analytics and tracking

Apart from the daily usage counts described above, the app contains no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no third-party scripts. The counts carry a random installation id, not your identity, and cannot be linked to you.

Deleting your data

Your data belongs to you and lives on your device. You can delete individual cards and photos in the app at any time. Removing all of it at once is best done by uninstalling the app or clearing its site data in your browser, which leaves nothing behind. (The app also has a Start over button, but it sits behind the owner's password and is not meant for everyday use; it deletes your cards, your photos, your progress and the app's own diagnostic logs, and keeps the settings — the language, the switches and any API key you entered.)

Children

The app is a general-audience learning tool for adults. It does not knowingly collect any personal information — from children or anyone else.
